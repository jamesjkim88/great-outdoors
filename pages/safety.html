Hiking Safety & Education

Whether you're a beginner or experienced hiker, safety should always be your top priority. Learn essential practices to make your Pacific Northwest adventures safe and enjoyable.

🎒

The Ten Essentials

Never hit the trail without these essential items. Even on short day hikes, conditions can change quickly in the Pacific Northwest.

🗺️

Navigation

Map, compass, and GPS device or app. Don't rely solely on your phone.

☀️

Sun Protection

Sunglasses, sunscreen, and hat. UV exposure is high at elevation.

🧥

Insulation

Extra clothing layers. Weather can change rapidly in the mountains.

🔦

Illumination

Headlamp or flashlight with extra batteries.

🩹

First Aid

Basic first aid kit with bandages, medications, and blister treatment.

🔥

Fire Starter

Matches, lighter, and fire starter in waterproof container.

🔧

Repair Kit

Knife or multi-tool, duct tape, and gear repair supplies.

🍎

Nutrition

Extra food beyond what you plan to eat. High-energy snacks.

💧

Hydration

Water and purification method. Drink before you feel thirsty.

⛺

Emergency Shelter

Emergency bivy, space blanket, or large garbage bags.

☁️

Seasonal Awareness

The Pacific Northwest experiences distinct seasons, each with unique challenges and considerations.

💧

Spring (March - May)

  • ✓ Rain: Expect wet conditions. Bring waterproof gear.
  • ✓ Snow: High elevation trails may still have snow.
  • ✓ Runoff: Rivers and streams are high.
  • ✓ Mud: Trails can be muddy and slippery.
☀️

Summer (June - August)

  • ✓ Heat: Start early to avoid midday heat.
  • ✓ Crowds: Popular trails are busiest.
  • ✓ Fire: Be aware of fire restrictions.
  • ✓ Water: Streams may be low. Carry extra.
🍂

Fall (September - November)

  • ✓ Weather: Conditions become unpredictable.
  • ✓ Daylight: Days get shorter. Bring headlamp.
  • ✓ Hunting: Wear bright colors during season.
  • ✓ Leaves: Trail may be obscured.
❄️

Winter (December - February)

  • ✓ Snow/Ice: Many trails require snowshoes.
  • ✓ Avalanche: Check forecasts for alpine areas.
  • ✓ Shorter Days: Very limited daylight.
  • ✓ Cold: Hypothermia risk. Dress in layers.
🐻

Wildlife Safety

Bears

Black bears are common in the Pacific Northwest. While attacks are rare, it's important to be prepared.

  • Make noise while hiking to avoid surprising bears
  • Carry bear spray and know how to use it
  • Store food properly and never approach or feed wildlife
  • If you see a bear, back away slowly and don't run

Cougars

Mountain lions are elusive but present in the region.

  • Hike in groups when possible
  • Keep children close and within sight
  • If you encounter a cougar, make yourself look large and don't run

Insects & Plants

  • Watch for ticks in grassy areas. Check yourself after hiking
  • Learn to identify poison oak and avoid contact
  • Bees and wasps are most active in late summer
🍃

Leave No Trace Principles

These seven principles help protect our natural areas and ensure they remain beautiful for future generations.

1

Plan Ahead and Prepare

Know the regulations, prepare for extreme weather, and schedule your trip to avoid times of high use.

2

Travel and Camp on Durable Surfaces

Stay on designated trails, camp on established sites, and keep campsites small.

3

Dispose of Waste Properly

Pack out all trash, deposit human waste properly, and wash dishes away from water sources.

4

Leave What You Find

Don't take rocks, plants, or artifacts. Leave areas as you found them for others to enjoy.

5

Minimize Campfire Impacts

Use a camp stove for cooking. If fires are permitted, use established fire rings and burn all wood to ash.

6

Respect Wildlife

Observe from a distance, never feed animals, and store food securely.

7

Be Considerate of Others

Keep noise down, yield to others on trails, and take breaks away from the trail.

📞

Emergency Preparedness

Before You Go

  • Tell someone where you're going and when you'll be back
  • Check weather forecasts and trail conditions
  • Download offline maps (cell service is often unavailable)
  • Fully charge your phone and consider bringing a backup battery

If You Get Lost

  • STOP: Stay calm, Think, Observe, Plan
  • Stay where you are if possible - it's easier for rescuers to find you
  • Make yourself visible with bright colors or reflective materials
  • Signal for help: three of anything (whistle blasts, fires, etc.) is the universal distress signal

Emergency Contacts

  • Emergency Services: 911
  • Non-Emergency Search & Rescue: Contact local sheriff's office
  • USFS Pacific Northwest Region: (503) 808-2468
